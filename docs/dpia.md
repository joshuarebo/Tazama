# Data Protection Impact Assessment — TAZAMA

Against the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 (DPA) and the Data Protection (General)
Regulations, 2021.

**Status: not signed off.** This is the build team's assessment of a system running on
synthetic data. It is a prerequisite document for a review by the data controller's Data
Protection Officer, not a substitute for one. `docs/tech-debt.md` #9 tracks this.

**The system has never processed real personal data.** Every figure, subscriber, complaint
and site in the demo is generated by `backend/src/tazama/infrastructure/db/seed.py` from a
fixed random seed. That is why this assessment can be specific about design and must be
provisional about operation.

---

## 1. Why an assessment is required

Section 31 of the DPA requires one where processing is likely to result in high risk. Four
triggers apply:

1. **Large-scale processing** — the subscriber base of a national mobile network.
2. **Systematic monitoring** — continuous correlation of complaints with network behaviour.
3. **Automated processing informing decisions** about individuals (whether to notify them,
   how to prioritise their complaint).
4. **Location data** — a complaint is resolved to a serving cell, which is location data at
   cell granularity.

---

## 2. The processing

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Controller | The mobile network operator |
| Processor | TAZAMA, deployed inside the operator's own environment |
| Purpose | Detect, explain and resolve network incidents faster, and tell affected customers before they have to call |
| Lawful basis | Legitimate interest in maintaining service quality, and performance of the subscriber contract (DPA s.30(1)(b)). **Not consent** — service assurance is not optional, which is precisely why the proactive-notification path has its own opt-out |
| Data subjects | Subscribers who contact care, and subscribers served by an affected cell |
| Retention | Operational data per the operator's own schedule; the app's own audit rows are append-only and retained for traceability |
| Cross-border transfer | **None by design.** The model endpoint, database and all adapters are inside the operator's network (`LLM_BASE_URL` is env-configured and the gateway is the only egress). Configuring a hosted model would be a transfer and requires its own assessment |

## 3. What is processed, and what each component actually sees

The central design decision: **pseudonymisation happens at the ingestion boundary**, so the
raw identifier exists only in the few lines of code that receive it.

| Data | Raw at ingestion | After the boundary | Seen by analytics | Seen by the model | Seen in a Teams card | In a log |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MSISDN | Yes, transiently | HMAC-SHA256 token (`SUB-…`) | Token only | **Never** | **Never** | Masked |
| Complaint text | Yes | Stored | Aggregated | Redacted | **Never** | Scrubbed |
| Stated location | Yes | Stored | Used for site identification | Redacted | **Never** | Scrubbed |
| Serving cell | Derived | Stored | Yes | Yes (cell id, not subscriber) | Cell counts only | Yes |
| Care-call reason | Yes | Stored | Aggregated | Aggregated | Counts only | Yes |
| Enterprise account ref | Yes | Stored | Yes | **Never** | Counts only | Masked |
| Subscriber segment, SLA tier | Derived | Stored | Yes | **Never** | **Never** | Yes |

Controls behind each "never":

- `ai/llm/redaction.py` redacts before the wire and raises on an identifier found in model
  output, in tool-call arguments, or in a repair attempt.
- `modules/reporting/digest.py` scans every card for MSISDN, token and e-mail shapes and
  **raises** rather than redacting — a silent scrub would hide the upstream leak.
- `core/logging.py` scrubs MSISDN shapes and long digit runs at the logging boundary.
- `api/v1/routers/*` run `scrub_text` over every free-text preview.

## 4. Data-subject rights

| Right (DPA) | How it is served | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access (s.26(a)) | Care lookup returns everything held about one subscriber on one screen | The export is per-lookup, not a formal subject-access package |
| Correction (s.26(c)) | Complaint classification and site identification are correctable, and every change is audited | No subscriber-facing correction route; it goes through care |
| Deletion (s.26(e)) | Deleting the token removes the subscriber from everything downstream, because the token *is* the identity | **No implemented erasure procedure.** See §7 |
| Objection to automated processing (s.35) | AI never decides. Prioritisation is a transparent rule with stored weights, and every customer-facing action requires human approval | — |
| Explanation of a decision | Every decision stores actor, inputs, rule version, confidence and evidence; the incident card renders them | — |
| Withdraw from proactive messages | `subscriber.opt_out`, honoured by the notification service and surfaced to the care agent | Opt-out is set operator-side, not self-service |

## 5. Risk assessment

Likelihood and impact are the build team's estimates on the current design.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Residual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Identifier reaches a model endpoint | Low | High | Pseudonymisation plus gateway redaction; outbound scan on body, tool arguments and repairs | Low |
| R2 | Identifier in a forwarded Teams card | Low | High | Card scan raises; no subscriber data in the digest by construction | Low |
| R3 | Re-identification from a cell-and-time combination | **Medium** | Medium | Analytics are aggregated and normalised; a small cell at a quiet hour is still potentially identifying | **Medium — see §7** |
| R4 | Subscriber enumeration through the care tooling | Low | Medium | `/care/samples` is demo-only; token lookup refused outside demo; every lookup is audited | Low |
| R5 | Over-notification becoming a nuisance | Medium | Low | Dedupe, quiet hours, rate limits, opt-out, human approval | Low |
| R6 | Wrong customer told their service is broken | Low | Medium | The known-issue claim is only made when the serving cell matches; a region-only match is explicitly flagged as weaker | Low |
| R7 | Insider misuse of care lookup | Medium | High | RBAC, append-only audit of every lookup | **Medium** — detection, not prevention |
| R8 | Salt compromise reversing pseudonyms | Low | High | Salt in env only, never logged, never in the repo | Low, but **no rotation procedure** |
| R9 | Model output misleading an engineer | Medium | Medium | Grounding checks discard ungrounded diagnoses and invented figures; confidence floors route to a human | Low |
| R10 | Analytics inheriting bias from suppression decisions | Medium | Medium | Exploration slice retained; decisions logged with versions; model cards state the limitation | Medium |

## 7. Required before any real data

These are blocking, not advisory.

1. **DPO sign-off** on this assessment.
2. **An erasure procedure.** Deleting a token must cascade to complaints, care calls,
   incident links and analytics, and the procedure has to be tested. Today there is none.
3. **A re-identification review of the insights surface** (R3). A per-cell complaint rate
   at 03:00 on a cell with twelve subscribers is close to identifying. The likely answer is
   a minimum-exposure floor below which a cell is not reported individually.
4. **A salt-rotation procedure** (R8), including what happens to historical analytics when
   the link is broken.
5. **Retention schedule agreed with the controller**, and implemented as a job rather than
   a document.
6. **An audit-review routine** for care lookups (R7). An append-only log that nobody reads
   is evidence after the fact, not a control.
7. **Reconcile the seeded KPI definitions with the operator's own counters**
   (`docs/tech-debt.md` #10) — not a privacy matter, but the assessment's assumptions about
   what the data means depend on it.

---

## 8. Conclusion

On the current design and with synthetic data, residual risk is **low to medium**, with the
medium entries concentrated in re-identification through aggregate analytics (R3), insider
misuse of legitimate access (R7), and feedback bias in the models (R10).

The design decisions that do most of the work are: pseudonymisation at the boundary rather
than in the analytics layer; a single egress chokepoint for model traffic; and the absence
of any execution path to the network, which removes a whole class of risk instead of
mitigating it.

The honest summary: the architecture is defensible, and the operational procedures —
erasure, rotation, retention, audit review — do not exist yet. Those are §7, and they are
required before a real subscriber record enters any environment.
